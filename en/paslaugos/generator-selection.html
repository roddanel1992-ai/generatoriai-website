Generator Selection for Homes

Generators for homes have their specific requirements. When selecting a generator for your home, it's important to consider not only the power needs but also noise level, safety, ease of use and long-term reliability. Our team specializes in generator selection and setup for homes.

What You Should Know When Selecting a Generator for Your Home?

  • Noise level: Generators for homes must be quiet so they don't disturb neighbors and your family. Inverter generators for homes are among the quietest (50-60 dB)
  • Power: Homes usually need 3-10 kW generator, depending on home size and needs
  • Automatic startup: Generators for homes should have an automatic transfer switch (ATS) so the generator automatically starts when power goes out
  • Location: Generators for homes can be installed outside, in garage or in a utility room
  • Fuel: For homes we usually use gasoline or inverter generators because they are quiet and easier to operate

How is Generator Selection Process Conducted?

1. Initial Conversation and Needs Analysis

By contacting us by phone or email, we will discuss:

  • Purpose of use – whether you need continuous power supply or just backup power
  • Facility type – private home, cottage, farm, business facility, industrial plant
  • Critical equipment – what must work even if power goes out (heating, refrigerator, servers)
  • Budget – how much you plan to invest in the generator and installation
  • Location for installation – where the generator will be installed (outdoors, garage, utility room)

2. Electricity Consumption Calculation

This is the most important step. We need to accurately calculate how much electrical power is needed:

  • Current appliances – complete list of all electrical devices and their power
  • Simultaneously operating devices – not all appliances run at the same time
  • Starting currents – motor devices (pumps, compressors) consume 2-7 times more power when starting
  • Power reserve – it's recommended to have 20-30% power reserve for the future
  • Voltage drop – long cables can reduce voltage, requiring higher power

3. Generator Type Selection

Based on your needs, we suggest the appropriate generator type:

Gasoline Generators

  • Advantages: Lower initial cost, lighter, quieter, simpler to operate
  • Disadvantages: Higher fuel consumption, shorter lifespan, not suitable for long-term work
  • Suitable for: Backup power, small power needs (up to 10 kW), rarely used facilities

Diesel Generators

  • Advantages: More economical to operate, longer lifespan, reliable for long-term work, larger power models
  • Disadvantages: Higher initial cost, louder, heavier, requires more frequent maintenance
  • Suitable for: Business facilities, industrial plants, large power needs (10 kW and above), frequent use

Inverter Generators

Inverter generators are the ideal choice for homes where quiet and high-quality power is needed. These generators use advanced technology that provides stable power output suitable for sensitive electronic devices.

  • Advantages: Very quiet (50-60 dB), high-quality power output (suitable for sensitive devices), compact, economical, automatic power regulation
  • Disadvantages: Most expensive, limited power (usually up to 7 kW)
  • Suitable for: Generators for homes, computers, medical equipment, audio systems, facilities with strict acoustic requirements
  • Inverter generators for homes: Ideal for private home, cottage and vacation home owners who value quiet operation and quality

4. Brand Comparison

We offer options from several reliable manufacturers:

  • Premium segment: Honda, Yamaha, Denyo – highest quality, quietest, most reliable
  • Mid-range: Hyundai, Kipor, Könner & Söhnen – good price-to-quality ratio
  • Budget segment: ELITECH, PATRIOT, Champion – affordable prices, sufficiently functional

5. Additional Criteria

We also consider:

  • Noise level – whether it's important that the generator is quiet (residential areas)
  • Automatic startup (ATS) – whether the generator should automatically start when power goes out
  • Mobile vs Stationary – whether the generator needs to be portable
  • Fuel tank – how long the generator can run without refueling
  • Safety – overload protection, automatic shutdown in case of problems

Common Mistakes When Selecting a Generator

We help avoid these common mistakes:

  • Choosing too low power – forgetting about starting current, power reserve
  • Wrong fuel type selection – gasoline is inefficient for long-term work
  • Ignoring noise – cheap generators can be very loud
  • Ignoring installation location – not all places are suitable due to ventilation, noise, safety
  • Lack of automation – manual startup is inconvenient, especially at night
  • Poor quality equipment – cheap generators break quickly, need frequent repairs
